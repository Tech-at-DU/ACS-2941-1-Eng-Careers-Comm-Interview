## Overview: A Different Skill, Same Stakes (10 min)

Technical rounds test whether you can build the right thing. Behavioral rounds test whether the team wants to work with you — and there's no single correct answer to grade against, which is exactly what makes them harder to prepare for. What interviewers are actually listening for is structure (did you tell a complete story) and specificity (was this really you, or a vague "we" accomplishment). AI tools are fine to use ahead of time to help draft and polish your STAR stories — nobody expects you to write these cold — but the interview itself is still just you, talking, live, with no tool open.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of today, you will be able to...

1. **Identify your own relevant experiences** — pull concrete stories from your background that map to common behavioral question categories
1. **Structure an answer using the STAR method** — situation, task, action, result, in that order, _without rambling_
1. **Answer common behavioral questions live**, including ones you didn't specifically prepare for
1. **Give structured feedback** on another candidate's behavioral answer, beyond "that was good"

## Warm-Up (15 min)

**Alone, 5 minutes:** list 3 real experiences (school, work, a group project, even something non-technical) you could tell a story about if asked "tell me about a time you...". Just the list — no full stories yet.

**Then, with a partner (10 min):** share your list. Does your partner see a question category you're missing — leadership, conflict, failure, initiative? Add one to your list if so.

## Activity 1: Behavioral Interview Prep (35 min)

1. Create your own Google Doc (or a file in your own repo, if you're fine with it being public) using the section headers from the [Behavioral Interview Prep Worksheet](../Assignments/Behavioral-Interview-Prep-Worksheet.md) as your starting template.
1. Fill it out with all the relevant info you need to have close to you when having the interview. This is personal — a Google Doc can stay private, you only need to share the link.
1. Submit the link via the [Assignment Link Submission form](https://forms.gle/o2rLExrzRuJWi7Px7) once you're done.
1. Optional to share with people who are also done to get feedback.

## Activity 2: Breakouts (1 hour 10 min)

**Before you start — STAR:**
- Situation — brief context, 1-2 sentences
- Task — what you specifically were responsible for
- Action — what you did, step by step (this is the longest part)
- Result — the outcome, ideally measurable, plus what you learned

Get together in groups of 3. You will rotate the following roles, about 20 minutes each:

**Recruiter**: Ask 2-3 questions from the Interviewee's own completed worksheet first (the doc they made in Activity 1 — have them pull it up, no need to copy anything) — questions they've already prepped. With about 5-8 minutes left in the rotation, ask one question **not** on their list, from the [Behavioral Question Bank](../Assignments/Behavioral-Question-Bank.md) instead — this is the one they haven't rehearsed.

**Interviewee**: Real interview scenario, make the best attempt at answering all the questions using STAR. Have your own worksheet doc open (from Activity 1) for reference during the known-question portion.

**Observer**: Fill out the Behavioral section of the [Peer Interview Rubric](../Assignments/Sample_Rubric.md) (details below) as you watch, for both the known and cold portions.

**This counts toward the Interviewee's 6-of-8 in-class peer interview rubrics.** Everybody shares observations and constructive feedback for 3-4 minutes after every rotation. **Then submit:** Observer submits via the [Peer Interview Rubric form](https://forms.gle/m8j2nLWB3HbgnGGx9), Interviewee submits their self-assessment via the [Self-Assessment form](https://forms.gle/6cWUACVPmBtaY4jV8) — note the lesson number and your partner's name on both. Then rotate roles.

## Debrief (10 min)

As a full class: which was harder — answering a question you'd already prepped for, or the cold one at the end? What made a STAR answer easy to follow when you were the Observer, versus one that was hard to track?

## Exit Ticket (10 min)

**Students:** individually, no partner — submit via the [Exit Ticket form](https://forms.gle/hLLsHySp2BT7qXAZ7) before you go.

> Which behavioral question category (teamwork, conflict, failure, initiative) do you currently have the weakest story for? Name one real experience you could turn into a STAR story before the next round.

**Instructor:** this tells you who needs to build out their story bank before Lesson 14's final mock interviews — worth a check-in if the same gaps show up across several students.

The worksheet you completed in class isn't the homework — this is new material, not a resubmission.
